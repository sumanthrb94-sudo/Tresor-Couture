What holds the score down is not software quality — it is three business exposures: an unresolved GST rate on the flagship product, a storefront invisible to Google, and two leaked credentials awaiting rotation. 3 · Risk register — what I would fix before spending a rupee on ads

R1 · GST rate on the flagship product — financial, accumulating per sale

The app charges a flat 5% GST. Since the Sept-2025 reform, garments above ₹2,500/piece attract 18%. If that applies to the ₹35,999 lehengas, each sale under-collects ≈ ₹3,777 that the firm still owes — ≈ ₹53,000 across the current 14-piece drop. Every invoice line also prints HSN 5007 (silk fabric), wrong for stitched garments. Status: awaiting CA confirmation by the owner's decision; code change is ready to start the moment rates are confirmed.

R2 · Exposed credentials — security, one bad actor from catastrophic

The Firebase service-account key (full database access) was shared into a chat transcript after rotation, and a live Razorpay Key Secret appeared in a screenshot. Rotating both is ~15 minutes in two dashboards. Nothing else on this list matters if these leak.

R3 · Invisible storefront — growth, compounding daily

One indexable URL means zero organic product traffic and, critically, no Google Shopping / Performance Max campaigns — Merchant Center requires real per-product URLs. The routing migration is the single engineering investment that unlocks both organic and paid product distribution.

R4 · Catalogue content — conversion, silent

48 of 50 products carry a critical defect: 43 share a description, 31 are named after stock codes ("HA3858"), 34 show a computed fake discount, 34 embed images as base64 (uncacheable, invisible to image search). A ₹35,999 purchase decision deserves better copy than a SKU.

R5 · Config not yet applied — 10 minutes of owner action

VITE_GSTIN (certificate issued 08/08/2026) and the full registered address (Gachibowli · 500046) are not yet in Vercel; Sentry DSN unset; Upstash rate-limit store unset. All are paste-and-redeploy.

Tier 0 · Free, this week

Ask every buyer at handover; put the review link in the order-confirmation email (template change, Brevo already live). Target: 15–25 genuine reviews in 60 days. Never incentivise or gate reviews — that risks suspension.
  • Fix the GBP description and categories (drafted earlier): it currently sells a "global fabric house", which excludes the profile from "fashion store near me" queries. Add In-store shopping under Service options; complete the address with PIN 500046.
  • Finish the profile: logo, cover, 15–25 photos, hours, Products section with the ₹35,999 pieces.

Tier 1 · Email automation — highest ROI paid tooling, mostly free tier

  • Implemented today: welcome on signup, order confirmation, admin bulk campaigns (XSS-guarded).
  • Build next, in order: abandoned-cart (fires 2–24h after an authenticated cart stalls; industry-typical recovery is meaningful at this price point), post-purchase review request (doubles as the GBP review engine), win-back at 90 days. All three run on the existing Brevo account; free tier is 300 emails/day, ample at current volume.

Tier 2 · Google Ads with the new-advertiser credit

  • The offer, precisely: India's current new-advertiser promotion is spend-matched — typically spend ₹20,000 within 60 days, receive ₹20,000 in credit (tiers up to ₹60k exist). It is not free money up front; it halves the effective cost of your first two months. New Ads accounts only, promo applied at signup.
  • Where to point it: Search campaigns on high-intent local queries — "bridal lehenga Hyderabad", "designer lehenga Gachibowli", "pre-draped saree Hyderabad" — landing on the shop. At a ₹35,999 order value, one conversion pays for the entire matched spend.
  • Prerequisites before the first rupee: revoke/replace the exposed Razorpay secret (you are about to drive real buyers), import GA4 purchase events as Ads conversions (events are already wired — this is configuration, not code), and accept that Shopping/PMax stays locked until R3 (real product URLs) is done.
  • Sequencing: run ads only after Tier 0 and the abandoned-cart email exist — paid traffic into a store with no reviews and no recovery loop is buying leaks.

Tier 3 · After the routing migration

  • Merchant Center + free Shopping listings + Performance Max; product schema for rich results; Instagram Shopping catalogue from the same feed.

6 · CEO's closing view

This platform is over-built in exactly the right place — trust. The security rules are the boundary and they are tested like it; the returns promise is enforced in the database; a fake discount or a fake tax number cannot reach a customer. That is what lets a two-person atelier take five-figure orders without an ops team, and it is why the engineering grade is honest, not generous.

The gap is entirely on the demand side. Today the store is a beautifully run shop on a street nobody walks down: one indexable URL, zero reviews, no recovery emails. The next ninety days should spend almost nothing on new features and everything on being found — reviews first because they are free, email automation second because it compounds, the ads credit third because it halves the cost of learning which queries convert, and the routing migration as the one real engineering project, because it unlocks every product-level channel at once.

Rating: 7.5/10 — launch, take real orders in a controlled way, fix R1 and R2 this week, and do not scale paid traffic until the ads prerequisites are met.
